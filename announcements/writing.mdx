# Writing Great Announcements

Your changelog is a direct line to your users. Here's how to make the most of it.

## The anatomy of a great announcement

### 1. Title

Your title is the first thing users see. Make it count.

**Do:**
- Be specific: "Export reports to PDF"
- Lead with the benefit: "Faster page loads"
- Use action words: "New", "Introducing", "Now available"

**Don't:**
- Be vague: "Updates" or "New feature"
- Use version numbers alone: "v2.4.1"
- Be too long: Keep it under 60 characters

### 2. Opening line

Hook readers in the first sentence. Get to the point immediately.

<CodeGroup>
```markdown Good
You can now export any report as a PDF with one click.
```
```markdown Bad
We've been working hard on this feature for months and we're excited to finally share it with you...
```
</CodeGroup>

### 3. Body content

Structure your content for scanning:

- **Use headings** to break up sections
- **Use bullet points** for lists of changes
- **Use bold text** for key information
- **Keep paragraphs short** — 2-3 sentences max

### 4. Visuals

A picture is worth a thousand words. Include:

- **Screenshots** for UI changes
- **GIFs** for interactive features
- **Code snippets** for API updates
- **Before/after** comparisons

<Tip>
  Use tools like [CleanShot](https://cleanshot.com) or [Loom](https://loom.com) to create polished screenshots and recordings.
</Tip>

## Writing for different announcement types

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="New Features">
    **Goal:** Get users excited and show them how to use it.

    **Structure:**
    1. What it is (one sentence)
    2. Why it matters (the benefit)
    3. How to use it (steps or link)
    4. What's next (optional)

    **Tone:** Excited but not over-the-top
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Improvements">
    **Goal:** Show you're listening and making things better.

    **Structure:**
    1. What changed
    2. Why (user feedback, data, etc.)
    3. The impact (faster, easier, etc.)

    **Tone:** Confident, appreciative of feedback
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Bug Fixes">
    **Goal:** Acknowledge the issue and confirm it's resolved.

    **Structure:**
    1. What was broken
    2. What's fixed now
    3. Thank reporters (if applicable)

    **Tone:** Direct, no excuses, grateful
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Writing tips from the pros

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Write for scanners">
    Most users won't read every word. Make key information impossible to miss with bold text, headings, and bullet points.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="One announcement, one topic">
    Don't bundle unrelated updates. Users should know exactly what an announcement is about from the title.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Link to more details">
    Keep announcements concise. Link to docs, blog posts, or help articles for users who want to go deeper.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Be human">
    It's okay to show personality. Thank users, admit mistakes, get excited about wins.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
